In my own words

Three different hats, one organisation.

I'm currently a research assistant with Democracy International's We The Peoples Campaign, helping identify which UN Member States could realistically join a potential "Group of Friends for Inclusive Global Governance" at the UN General Assembly. Most of my time on this goes into working through Freedom House democracy ratings, UN reform positioning, and multilateral engagement records, then building the case for two candidate countries, Chile and India.

Earlier this year I also stepped into a very different role for a few days, workshop facilitator. I designed and delivered a session called "Hello, I See You: Building Trust, Resilience and Advocacy", a 45-minute interactive talk followed by 15 minutes of hands-on activity, for 20 participants from different parts of the world. A lot of it drew on organisational psychology I studied at Hochschule Mittweida under Jill Deschner-Warner, looking at how cultural background shapes trust, and how something as small as making eye contact and offering a handshake can break down a barrier in seconds.

I'm also one of six co-authors of the Youth Participation Handbook, a guide Democracy International put together to help young people get politically active, produced with their team and edited by Lia Civolani.
